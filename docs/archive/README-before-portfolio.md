# CampusWall

CampusWall is a shared university noticeboard. Guests can select ANU, USYD, UNSW, UniMelb, Monash, UQ, UWA or Adelaide and browse published posters, event details and comments without registering. Students sign in only when they want to contribute.

## What good means here

A campus poster should communicate its event, date, location and purpose in a few seconds. CampusWall prioritises clarity, discovery and collaboration. Eight editable starting layouts keep creation approachable: minimal, photo cover, club ticket, academic editorial, social split, gallery frame, bold statement and gradient.

The project draws on Parimal Satyal's discussion of spaces made by and for ordinary people in [Rediscovering the Small Web](https://neustadt.fr/essays/the-small-web/). Posters are not ranked by an engagement algorithm, and visitors do not need a profile to browse.

## Using the wall

Each campus includes 24 photographic **test events** (192 campus records using 24 distinct photographs), with eight composition styles: concert covers, editorial spreads, film-style frames, arches, split layouts, tickets, sport typography and magazine covers. Images are served locally as optimised WebP assets. Every graphic and card has an English **TEST EVENT** label. Details explain that the activity and date are fictional, that no venue is booked, and that the stock photo does not depict the named campus venue. Real venue names have official university source links and named-venue map searches. Photographer credits and the [Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/) are provided on details; the complete references are in [photo poster sources](data/PHOTO-POSTER-SOURCES.md).

Calendar exports of test events prefix their titles with **[TEST]** and remain tentative, transparent entries. The seed upgrades only the six exact original built-in samples while preserving their URLs, IDs, comments and saved references. It adds the remaining test posters transactionally with stable identifiers, leaves other legacy and student notices intact, and does not reset dates on restart. Dates begin the day after first installation in each campus timezone. This collection does not imply university endorsement or a real event programme.

Guests choose a university on their first visit; later visits remember it locally, and guests can switch campuses. Each signed-in account has one fixed university, chosen during registration. Existing accounts confirm their university once before continuing. Signing in restores the account's university regardless of the previously browsed campus. The header shows the fixed school as text; direct links to another campus return to the account's own wall or clubs page, and server APIs and WebSocket handshakes reject cross-campus access. Upcoming events appear by default. Filter by category, today, this week, all dates or past events, or search event titles, locations and descriptions. Date badges distinguish today, tomorrow and past dates; they do not infer a finish time. Posters show event details alongside the visual design. The initial wall includes clearly identified sample notices.

Each university has a corresponding interface theme, applied when switching campuses and retained on poster details and the editor: ANU black/white with gold highlights, USYD charcoal with ochre, UNSW black/yellow, UniMelb blue/white, Monash blue/white, UQ purple/white, UWA navy/gold, and Adelaide navy/sky blue. These themes reference the universities' web identities ([ANU colour guide](https://webpublishing.anu.edu.au/web-style-guide/colours), [Sydney website](https://www.sydney.edu.au/), [UNSW website](https://www.unsw.edu.au/), [Melbourne colour accessibility guide](https://www.unimelb.edu.au/accessibility/tools/accessible-text-colors), [Monash](https://www.monash.edu/), [UQ](https://www.uq.edu.au/), [UWA](https://www.uwa.edu.au/), [Adelaide University](https://adelaide.edu.au/)). Adelaide uses the current 2026 university identity. Poster artwork retains the palette selected by its creator.

The header displays the selected university's original logo using locally served assets from university websites and public design resources. Monash's original monochrome mark uses a documented Wikimedia Commons mirror. It follows the fixed account university after sign-in and the selected campus for guests, including clubs, detail and editor pages. Source URLs are recorded in `public/logos/SOURCES.md`; the university marks are kept in their original colours and proportions. CampusWall remains a student project.

Registration validates email syntax and uses recognised university email domains to select and lock the account school. An ANU address ending in `@anu.edu.au` can register only for ANU; all eight schools support their published student-address domains and documented aliases. Domain matching is exact, case-insensitive and shared by the UI and server; lookalike domains such as `anu.edu.au.example.com` do not match ANU. Editing the email recalculates the selection, and the server rejects a different school even when the selector is bypassed. Personal emails retain manual university selection. Unassigned legacy accounts with recognised university emails confirm only that school. Existing fixed affiliations, passwords and sessions remain unchanged. This is email-domain routing, not mailbox ownership or student-status verification. See [email domain sources](data/EMAIL-DOMAINS.md).

Select Create Poster to sign in or register, choose a template and create a private draft. The publishing university is fixed to the account's university and shown as text, without another campus selector. New drafts can use their university’s signature poster palette. Edit event fields, template, colour palette and alignment, select a suggested campus venue or enter your own, and upload a JPEG, PNG or WebP image (up to 5 MB and 16 megapixels) or use an HTTPS image URL. Changes save automatically when the server accepts them. A connection indicator distinguishes saving from saved or reconnecting.

Signed-in visitors can save published events from their university to an account-specific list. Details provide an ICS calendar download, a public link and a downloadable QR code. Timed calendar entries and date badges use the campus timezone: Sydney for ANU/USYD/UNSW, Melbourne for UniMelb/Monash, Brisbane for UQ, Perth for UWA and Adelaide for Adelaide. Calendar exports include the applicable daylight-saving rules, including Adelaide's half-hour offset and Brisbane/Perth's fixed offsets. Events without a time become all-day entries. No end time is invented. Campus map links lead to university directories.

Every campus homepage includes its own live chat. Guests can read the selected university's history and receive its new messages; signed-in accounts can read and send only in their fixed university's channel. The author and university always come from the server session, and messages are displayed as plain text. HTTP history and live socket delivery are both scoped by university. The widget shows that university's latest 100 messages in order, retains history after refresh or server restart, and reloads it after reconnecting. Enter sends a message and Shift + Enter adds a line; Chinese input composition is respected. Messages are limited to 500 characters and 20 sends per account per minute. Retrying an interrupted request cannot create a duplicate message. Readers who scroll back get a new-message button instead of being forced to the bottom.

The chat also includes 20 curated campus prompts in a separate Campus broadcast panel. One prompt scrolls into view every six seconds, with Pause/Play and Next controls. Hovering, keyboard focus and a hidden browser tab pause rotation; reduced-motion preferences start it paused. Broadcasts carry no user identities or message timestamps and are never inserted into the user-message database or history.

Select Messages in the navigation to open your private inbox. Sign in, search a username or display name (at least two characters), choose a person and start a one-to-one conversation. Each account has a unique public username shown in its inbox; existing accounts receive one automatically and new registrations can choose an optional 3–24 character username using letters, numbers or underscores. Duplicate display names remain distinguishable. Search excludes your own account and legacy/system accounts, and does not expose email addresses.

Published poster cards and details also provide Message organiser, opening a conversation with the poster's actual creator without searching. Guests sign in and return to that conversation. The server resolves the creator from the published poster, reuses an existing conversation between the same accounts, and does not send an automatic message. Your own posters and notices owned by disabled/system accounts have no contact button. Private, unpublished or deleted posters cannot start conversations through this route.

Private messages are visible only to the conversation's two participants. The inbox lists the latest 100 conversations with message previews and unread counts. Opening a conversation loads its latest 50 messages; Load earlier messages retrieves older history. Messages are stored on the persistent volume and delivered live to both participants while their inbox is open. Reconnection retrieves missed messages in order, including more than one history page. Enter sends, Shift + Enter adds a line, and Chinese input composition is respected. Messages are plain text, limited to 2,000 characters and 30 sends per account per minute across all conversations. Unique request IDs prevent duplicate retries. Read positions sync across the reader's inbox tabs and advance only while viewing the bottom of a focused conversation. The mobile interface provides a conversation list and a back link from each chat.

The creator can invite a registered account from the same university by email from Collaborators. The invited poster appears under that account's My posters page. Collaborators can edit; only the creator can manage collaborators, publish, unpublish or delete. Publication requires a title, valid date and location. Published posters keep a deliberate snapshot: subsequent working-copy edits need republishing.

## The common room

Select Games in the header or campus navigation to open the ten-game lobby: Gomoku, Connect Four, Tic-tac-toe, Rock/Paper/Scissors, Memory Match, Battleship, Reversi, Draw & Guess, Campus Quiz and Chess. Guests can explore the game cards and rules; playing requires a signed-in account with a fixed university. Filter by quick play, strategy or social/creative games, or search game names and descriptions. The interface follows the campus palette, adapts to phones and animates page/card entrances, dropped pieces, card flips and result reveals. Reduced-motion preferences disable these animations.

Create an open table for another student at your university, or search for a campus friend and reserve the second seat. Copy the room link or explicitly send an invitation through Messages. The private conversation has a Play together link, and invitation messages have a Join game room link. Creating a room does not automatically send a message. Both players must join and mark themselves ready. The room displays connection status, online presence, the current turn, rules and saved match history. Reloading or reconnecting restores the authoritative board; an active match can be resigned and a rematch requires agreement from both players.

Gomoku uses a freestyle 15-by-15 board with five or more stones winning. Connect Four applies gravity to a seven-by-six board. RPS ends at two round wins, with ties not scoring. Memory uses eight randomly shuffled pairs, with mismatches revealed for 1.6 seconds before changing turns. Battleship uses an eight-by-eight ocean and three ships of lengths four, three and two; placement can be manual or random, ships cannot overlap, and sinking all nine opponent ship cells wins. Reversi captures in all eight directions and automatically passes when necessary. Chess uses chess.js 1.4.0 for legal moves, castling, en passant, all four promotions, checkmate, stalemate and automatic draw conditions, retaining full PGN history for repetition checks. Chess has no turn clock. Draw & Guess is cooperative: four 60-second rounds alternate the artist, accept English words and synonyms and award both players a point for a correct guess. Quiz uses eight questions with 20-second deadlines and correct-answer speed bonuses. No game involves money or prizes.

Game actions are validated on the server and saved before being broadcast. University scope and player membership apply to HTTP and WebSockets; only the two participants can enter a started match. Personalised responses hide unseen memory cards, the opponent fleet, the artist's word from the guesser and unrevealed RPS/quiz choices. Simultaneous independent actions are accepted within the same match/round, while old-round actions and stale board moves are rejected. Request IDs make interrupted retries idempotent. Drawing strokes use normalised coordinates and bounded points; completed strokes are sent live, and ongoing input is preserved when the opponent updates the canvas. Server timers advance rounds even if both tabs are closed.

## Architecture and persistence

All eight campuses have public clubs directories, checked on 1 October 2026, with 1,905 clubs in total: [ANU](/anu/clubs) contains 236 clubs in 16 categories from [ANUSA](https://anusa.com.au/clubs/clubs-list/); [USYD](/usyd/clubs) contains 301 clubs in 9 categories from [USU](https://usu.edu.au/clubs/); [UNSW](/unsw/clubs) contains 458 clubs in 16 categories from [Arc UNSW](https://www.arc.unsw.edu.au/clubs/find-a-club); [UniMelb](/unimelb/clubs) contains 246 clubs in 10 categories from [UMSU](https://umsu.unimelb.edu.au/buddy-up/clubs/clubs-listing/); [Monash](/monash/clubs) contains 114 clubs in 7 categories from [MSA Clayton](https://clubs.msa.monash.edu/joinnow/clubs-and-societies/); [UQ](/uq/clubs) contains 221 clubs in 14 categories from [UQ Union](https://uqu.com.au/clubs-and-societies/); [UWA](/uwa/clubs) contains 160 clubs in 23 categories from [UWA Student Guild](https://www.uwastudentguild.com/clubs); and [Adelaide](/adelaide/clubs) contains 169 clubs in 4 categories from [AUSA](https://www.ausaadelaide.com.au/community/clubs-societies/clubslist/). Monash's snapshot covers the published MSA Clayton directory. Counts reflect the actual public listings rather than marketing estimates. Each directory supports name/interest search, source categories, gradual loading and original public profile links. UMSU's published interest keywords are also searchable. Source logos appear where supplied; other clubs use initials. Guests switching universities from a clubs page open the corresponding directory and theme; signed-in accounts stay on their university.

The clubs page opens with 11 visible interest groups: sport/outdoors, technology/science, arts/performance, culture/languages, study/faculties, career/networking, games/hobbies, volunteering/community, faith/spirituality, politics/advocacy and social/campus life. Choose an interest before browsing club cards, search directly, or explicitly choose All clubs. Counts update with keyword and original-category filters. A club may belong to several interests, but is listed only once in each result. Interest groups combine source-category mappings with specific public club names; original directory categories remain on the cards. Every club belongs to at least one interest group. Search and selection are kept in the URL for refresh, sharing and campus changes. Results show the visible count, load 24 cards at a time, and provide a Change interest control.

These are directory references, separate from event posters; they do not create club accounts, announce events or grant membership. Saved snapshots are served locally without querying source sites on each visit. Refresh only when requested: `node scripts/import-anusa-clubs.mjs` refreshes ANU; `node scripts/import-campus-clubs.mjs` refreshes USYD/UNSW/UniMelb; `node scripts/import-go8-clubs.mjs` refreshes Monash/UQ/UWA/Adelaide. The latter two accept selected campus slugs, parse published directories without executing source scripts, validate campus scope, unique identities, complete pagination and source categories, and replace saved snapshots only after all selected sources validate successfully. UWA follows all published pagination pages; UQ reads its embedded anonymous directory API. Clubs listed in multiple categories appear once with all categories retained.

A small Node.js 24 HTTP service serves browser JavaScript and CSS. SQLite lives at `/data/campuswall.sqlite` in deployment, on the course-managed persistent Fly volume. Its versioned migration creates users, universities, posters, collaborator relationships, comments and sessions, with foreign keys and uniqueness constraints. A second additive migration adds uploaded-image metadata and saved events; a third adds public chat messages with ordered IDs and unique send requests. A fourth adds unique usernames, private conversations, messages and monotonic read positions while preserving existing accounts, passwords and sessions. The fifth adds a nullable account university and a database trigger preventing changes after selection. The sixth adds university scope to public chat messages. The seventh adds the four new universities idempotently without changing existing affiliations, posters, sessions or messages. The eighth adds durable two-player game rooms and unique action requests without changing existing records. Existing accounts choose explicitly; browser history is never used to assign their affiliation. Earlier shared chat messages remain stored with a null university and are excluded from campus channels because their school was not recorded. Existing passwords, sessions, posters and private messages are retained. Image files also live beneath `/data`. No separate database server is required.

The old `/data/poster-studio.json` file is not deleted. On first migration, original records are copied to a legacy archive and published legacy notices are converted to structured records. Anonymous legacy drafts remain archived because their display-name owners cannot be securely matched to a new account.

Passwords use salted scrypt hashes. Sessions use random HTTP-only cookies with seven-day expiry and store only token hashes in SQLite. Production cookies are Secure over Fly's HTTPS proxy. Private HTTP and WebSocket operations check account access on the server. Origin checks, request limits, field validation and safe text rendering protect the normal contribution paths.

WebSocket edits send a single whitelisted field and client version. The server checks permissions, persists the accepted field, increments the version and broadcasts authoritative state. Different-field edits survive together; the last accepted update wins for the same field. Very stale updates are rejected with the latest state. Reconnect reloads the saved state. Presence shows which field each collaborator is editing, with short-lived hints and heartbeat cleanup, and removing a collaborator immediately closes their editing connections.

Uploaded images are decoded, resized to at most 1600 pixels on either side, stripped of metadata and re-encoded as WebP. Only creators and current collaborators can fetch private images. Public visitors see only the image in the published snapshot; a replacement stays private until republishing. Processing is limited to one image at a time, with 20 stored images per poster and a 200 MB total upload cap for the course volume. Deleting a poster also removes its uploaded files.

## Run locally

```powershell
$env:DATA_DIR = Join-Path (Get-Location) '.local-data'
$env:PORT = '8080'
pnpm start
```

Open `http://localhost:8080`. In another terminal run `pnpm check` (set `APP_URL` if using another port). ## Performance and code organisation

The interface has a shared JavaScript motion layer: page and scroll entrances, staggered cards, click ripples, responsive filter/save feedback, photo tilt on fine-pointer devices, animated dialogs, new-message entrances, input focus feedback and a request progress indicator. Feedback does not postpone API calls or report success before the server accepts an action. Reduced-motion preferences disable movement, touch devices skip tilt, background tabs cancel motion, and history animations are capped. Three lifecycle checks cover failures, overlapping requests and reduced-motion dialog closure.

Poster lists retrieve their authors, comment totals and viewer-specific saves in one query. A bounded prepared-statement cache reuses SQL without caching permission decisions or query results. Public assets support Brotli/gzip and conditional requests; public photographs stream from disk. Games and messaging load on demand. Club search metadata is precomputed, and shared escaping, database initialization, poster presentation and public asset serving have dedicated modules. Unreferenced legacy code and duplicate fresh-install samples have been removed; existing data imports and additive migrations remain.

Eight additional regression checks cover query freshness, list isolation, compression, validators, HEAD, streamed photos and cache permission boundaries. The complete suite now covers 109 focused product scenarios plus the original course checks. See [performance evidence](data/PERFORMANCE.md) for the local before/after measurements and limitations. Use `pnpm benchmark current` with an isolated temporary database to reproduce the optimized benchmark.

## Scope and limitations

University selection does not verify university membership. The MVP uses registered email/password accounts without email verification, password recovery or outbound invitation emails. Invitations are in-app access grants. Private inbox delivery is live while the Messages page is open; unread messages are retained for the next visit. There are no push notifications, attachments, group chats, blocking tools or end-to-end encryption. Hero images support uploads and external HTTPS links. If no image is supplied, templates use a built-in graphic. Single-machine SQLite and room broadcasting fit the course deployment; scaling to multiple machines would require shared persistence and messaging.

An interrupted connection discards unaccepted browser edits with a visible message and reloads the server state. There is no offline editing queue. Real student use is still needed to judge readability and whether groups feel ownership of the posters they make.

## Shared design studio

Create poster opens eight visual starting points and a working title. The studio has Event, Design, Elements and Images tabs, with four typefaces, title sizing and positioning, campus palettes or custom colours, image crop/zoom/opacity, optional labels, borders, decoration, an extra text block and a movable sticker. Drag the title, callout or sticker on the canvas; arrow keys move focused elements and Shift increases the step. Zoom is local to each viewer. Print / Save PDF uses the browser print dialog after pending changes have saved. Existing posters retain their previous renderer until a design field is edited.

Owners can search registered same-campus friends by name or username, or invite by email. Copy workspace link shares the address; it grants no access on its own. Invited friends find the poster under My posters and edit live, with online avatars and editing-field indicators. Field edits merge through the existing authenticated WebSocket protocol and survive restarts. Undo/redo applies to the current user's session and refuses to overwrite a field changed by another collaborator. Only the owner can publish; public viewers continue to see the last published snapshot.

## Collaborative whiteboard

The default studio is now a shared free canvas with a dot-grid workspace, independent camera pan/zoom and touch pinch zoom. Choose Collaborative whiteboard for an empty 2400 × 1600 board, or convert an existing poster without replacing its public snapshot. Add multiple text blocks, sticky notes, rectangles, circles, arrows, lines, photographs and freehand strokes. Move each object, resize with its corner, rotate using its top handle, edit typography/colours/opacity, duplicate, lock and reorder layers. Brainstorm and planning shortcuts add editable notes. Canvas dimensions/background are configurable; up to 180 objects and 20 uploaded images per draft keep documents bounded. Objects can sit beyond the frame, but publishing and PNG export show the canvas frame.

Invited same-campus accounts share the saved objects, live pointers, selection outlines and transient movement previews. Each durable change is a validated object operation, never a client replacement of the whole board. Different-object and different-property edits merge; same-property changes follow server acceptance order. Save state is acknowledged by the server. Undo/redo verifies expected properties before changing them, preserving edits made by friends. Reconnection restores saved state; unsaved interrupted gestures are discarded. Event details remain separate listing metadata and owners retain exclusive publishing rights. PNG export embeds uploaded photographs; external photos need browser cross-origin permission and should be uploaded if export fails.

## Watch together

Open Watch together in the header, name a screening room and paste a YouTube link or a public HTTPS MP4, WebM or m3u8 video URL. Signed-in members of the same university can join through the lobby or copied room link. The screen sits beside an emoji-enabled chat, with online members' avatars/names below. Hosts control play, pause, seeking, video replacement and room closure. Viewers control their own sound, fullscreen and danmaku visibility. New chat messages optionally scroll over the video; history never replays as danmaku. Reduced motion suppresses scrolling and hidden pages cancel animations.

Playback uses a server timestamp and revision, with drift correction, late-join state and reconnection. Video starts muted for browser autoplay rules; Enable playback handles devices that require a gesture. Live streams can have different provider/buffering delays and cannot guarantee frame-exact synchronisation. YouTube embedding restrictions, expired URLs, browser codecs and HLS CORS still apply. Paid/login-only streaming pages are unsupported. Media goes directly from its provider to viewers: the app never downloads or proxies a supplied link. HLS support loads only when needed; YouTube uses the official IFrame API.

Additive migration 011 stores room clocks, request receipts and messages on the persistent volume. Author/campus/host identities are derived from the current session. Rooms are limited to 50 people and three tabs per account, hosts to five open rooms, messages to 500 characters and 20 per minute; room history and active animations are bounded. Only watch documents permit external playback through their scoped content security policy; entering from the main wall uses document navigation to apply it. `node --test scripts/watch.integration.mjs` verifies source validation, room isolation, real WebSocket presence, host controls/revisions/retries, chat limits, persistence and session revocation.


### Playback controls and buffering
The host can use either the room controls or the YouTube player's own play/pause button. Both update the saved room state; commands initiated by synchronisation are not echoed back as host actions. Buffering suppresses repeated play/seek commands. The host's actual playhead continues naturally and reanchors the room after drift, while viewers follow the shared clock. Provider end events pause the room only when the host is actually near the end. Player regression checks cover embedded controls across 45 timer ticks, buffering recovery, false/genuine endings, delayed acknowledgements and viewer control isolation.


### Closing a screening room
Hosts can close their room directly from its lobby card, or use Close room beside the player controls. Other users see no close action and the server rejects their control requests. Lobby closure fetches the latest room revision, prevents duplicate clicks through live card updates, and reports success only after server acceptance. Closed rooms disappear from live lobby lists; current viewers receive closure and their viewing connections end. Saved room/chat records are retained rather than deleted.


### Connected campus life
Campus life links ten new areas: organiser bookings/check-in, in-app notifications, an interactive activity map, followed-club student communities, team applications, accepted friends and private group chats, scheduled screenings and shared playlists, a personal campus calendar, game history/leaderboards and event insights. Existing posters, university access rules and production records remain intact through additive migration 012.

Organisers explicitly open registration and choose capacity/duration. Bookings are idempotent, excess guests wait in order, cancellation promotes the next guest, and only organisers can read guest lists or validate private QR ticket codes. Confirmed attendees enter a private event group; cancelling removes their group membership. Fictional photo test notices cannot accept bookings or imply attendance. View counts represent distinct signed-in non-owner visitors per day. Announcement notifications go only to booked/waitlisted guests.

Friend requests require acceptance. Manual groups accept only existing friends, and every group API rechecks membership; removal immediately ends access. Team organisers accept applications within capacity before exposing the private conversation. Club follows are visible to campus members; discussions and linked activities are student contributions, not verified official announcements or club membership. Official directory links remain available. Search never exposes emails.

Map markers use organiser-saved coordinates, selected by clicking the map or entering coordinates. Unpinned activities show their venue and a map-search link rather than invented precise positions. Leaflet is hosted locally; visible OpenStreetMap tiles load directly with attribution and no offline prefetching. The calendar merges confirmed bookings, saved tentative events and subscribed/hosted screenings in each campus timezone, identifies overlapping confirmed plans, and exports ICS. Event duration is organiser-configured, defaulting to 60 minutes until changed.

Scheduled screenings support subscriptions and in-app start reminders (checked once per minute). Hosts can start a screening, invite campus members, make a room invitation-only, transfer hosting to another online member and choose the next top-voted playlist video. Votes are unique per account/video. Room URLs do not bypass invitation checks. Game results are saved once per room/match from server-validated completion, including rematches; profiles show badges and a campus leaderboard offers the past seven days or all time. Only the last completed match can be recovered for rooms created before this feature. Notifications contain a short summary and link, never private message bodies, and remain private to the recipient.


### Private voice calls
Open a private conversation and select **Voice call** beside the recipient. Both users must be online, signed in and on the same fixed campus. The recipient receives an Answer/Decline panel across pages. Calling or answering asks the browser for audio-only microphone access; incoming rings do not capture audio. Calls support microphone mute, local playback enablement, elapsed duration, cancellation and hangup. Changing application pages keeps the audio controller alive; signing out, leaving the document or ending a call stops capture. A second tab cannot take control of an answered call. Missed calls expire after 35 seconds; unsuccessful connection setup expires after 45 seconds.

WebRTC carries audio between participants. This application records only call lifecycle metadata; it does not record audio or persist SDP/ICE. The authenticated social channel relays bounded audio-only signals to the selected participant device/session. Default ICE uses a public STUN service. A TURN relay is **not configured by default**: restrictive NAT/firewall networks can fail to connect. Configure an authenticated private TURN service through the Fly secret `VOICE_ICE_SERVERS`, containing a JSON RTCIceServer array (STUN/TURN URLs and any required username/credential). Never commit relay credentials. Microphone access requires HTTPS and a supported browser.

UI typography uses a shared system sans-serif scale for navigation, messages, campus tools, profiles and room titles. Poster artwork retains its chosen design fonts. Secondary navigation lives under **Explore**; phone chat inputs use a readable 16 px font.
