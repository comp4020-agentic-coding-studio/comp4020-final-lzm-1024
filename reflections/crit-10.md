# Crit 10 — working features still need clear starting points

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The useful breakthrough was separating three questions: does a feature work, can somebody find it, and what can the logs prove?

On 14 October 2026, my friends Tony and Kevin tried the website and reported YouTube stutter. I also needed to introduce some features before they knew those features were available. The website offers many functions, but a first-time visitor does not already know what to look for. This showed that making a feature work and making it easy to discover are different jobs. A clearer starting point could reduce the need for my explanation.

In the Week 11 tutorial, I also completed an instruments-only demonstration with Group 3. Four people took part. We shared the website link, posters and video rooms, and tested games. Language and video problems appeared. I have not supplied individual log events or examples of correct and incorrect narration, so I cannot claim a measured level of accuracy.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to check both technical behaviour and first-use understanding. Instead of only adding features, I should help people discover what is already there. Clearer labels, a short optional introduction or a few starting tasks are possible next steps, not improvements that have already been tested.

Logs can distinguish actors and accepted or rejected requests without exposing private text. They cannot show whether a translation makes sense or a video looks smooth. My account needs concrete comparisons between narration and actual actions; those details are still missing. Later technical repairs are separate evidence, and no participant retest has confirmed that the reported problems are solved.
